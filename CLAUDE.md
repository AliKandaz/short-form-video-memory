## 1. What we learned from the source video

**Source:** "How A Branch Of Bamboo Can Turn Into A Nipa Hut in the jungle"
(https://www.tiktok.com/@howbuildingexplained/video/7703769950413933854). 66.6 s, 7.2M views,
731K likes, 18.4K shares. It was cross-checked against the account's #2 video ("How A Hole In Your Yard Can Become A Levee",
100 s, 1.9M views). Both follow the identical formula.

### The niche
- 60–110 s fully 3D-animated vertical explainers. A humble starting thing becomes an impressive structure,
  one problem and one fix at a time, narrated in second person ("you").
- Title formula: `How [humble thing] Can Become [impressive / famous / survival structure] (in the [setting])`
  plus `#engineering #HowItWorks #Science #LearnOnTikTok` and 1–2 topic tags.
- What wins on the account: **primitive survival builds** (jungle nipa hut 7.2M, Amazon floating house 769K)
  and **famous or high-stakes structures** (levee 1.9M, White House 1.6M, nuclear bunker 1.4M, Kremlin 1.0M,
  nuclear plant 976K, particle accelerator 900K).
- What flops: mundane utilities (car wash 9.5K, slot machine 10K, septic 25K, drive-thru 30K, oil well 41K) and
  the "X-proof house" series (fireproof 131K, volcano 95K, earthquake 79K). The igloo also did poorly (38K).
- The hit is one of the shortest videos. Tight scripts with more danger per second do better.

### Script formula
```
HOOK (0-3s)  If [you're in situation], [do a naive thing] and you have a [crude version].
LOOP x6-8    But [specific problem].  ->  So you [concrete fix].  ->  (This is / These are [REAL TERM].)
TWIST        But the deadliest [X] here isn't [obvious thing]. It's [surprise]. + one shock stat
ASIDE        And [fun fact]  /  That's why [thing viewers have seen in real life].
CLIMAX       But then [worst-case disaster] hits. [Everything around you is destroyed.]
PAYOFF       But yours [stands / stays] [safe] while [disaster happens right next to you].
COZY         (You) add [prop 1], [prop 2] and [prop 3].
SIGN-OFF     Congratulations, you have built a [X].      <- their signature line; we never use it (our ending: section 5)
```

### Writing rules
- Second person, present tense. The viewer is always the builder ("you lift", "you drive", "you raise").
- One action or one problem per sentence. Short, mostly one-syllable words, no filler, no hedging.
- Every "But" escalates: nuisance -> weather -> hidden killer -> night threat -> catastrophe.
- After each fix, name the real-world term ("These are stilts." "This is thatch." "This is a rat guard.").
  The term also appears as an ALL-CAPS on-screen label.
- Use everyday and body-scale comparisons: "to your shoulders", "as high as a basketball hoop",
  "knee-high by morning", "hard as rock". **Ours since 11 Nov 2026 (3.4 shot rule):** a comparison is shown only as a label or a
  measurement line on the build; cut any that would need a picture of something else.
- At least one shock stat ("kills more people than snakes, sharks and lions combined", "84% fewer").
  **Fact-check every number before production.**
- One "that's why" tie-in ("That's why levees on the news are wrapped in plastic."). Ours: shown on the build or as a label (3.4).
- Pace: about 250 words per 66 s (~3.8 words/s, ~225 wpm). Long versions run ~100 s and ~350 words.

### Animation style
- **Look:** full 3D CG with a real-time, game-engine feel. Semi-realistic textures on simple models, soft shadows,
  mild depth of field. Not photoreal, not cartoon-shaded.
- **Set:** one small diorama (jungle clearing, suburban lawn) that stays the same location while the structure
  evolves on it.
- **Character:** one generic stock-rigged man (olive-green jumpsuit, no facial animation) for scale and
  simple poses: lying, standing, arms over head, waving. Machinery (excavator, backhoe, roller) can replace him.
- **Mood through grading:** daylight for fixes, dark desaturated for night threats, blue-grey for storms, sunny
  for the ending.
- **Camera:** about one shot per sentence (2–5 s), hard cuts on "But" and "So", slow push-ins and orbits within shots,
  macro close-ups of key details and then a pull back to the wide shot. **We dropped the one-shot-per-sentence part on 11 Nov 2026:**
  our videos stay on one build in one main view (3.4 shot rule, 7.2).
- **Build moments:** parts appear, slide or grow into place exactly when the narrator names them.
- **Problems** are shown with environmental effects: rain particles, a rising water plane, critter models, swarm particles,
  darkness.
- **Explainer devices** for invisible mechanisms: cross-section cutaways, magnifying-glass macro, x-ray pipes, blue water
  particles, red and blue force arrows, a yellow highlight ring, dashed measurement lines ("10 FT").
- **Captions:** ONE word at a time, synced to the voice, centred at ~52% frame height. Title Case, heavy rounded sans
  (Fredoka-like), white fill, thin black outline, soft drop shadow.
- **Labels:** ALL CAPS bold white sans with shadow, placed by the object, for the term or stat
  (STILTS, THATCH, DEADLIEST ANIMAL, 84% FEWER, RAT GUARD).
- **Format:** 9:16, 30 fps, 720×1280 or 1080×1920, 60–110 s.
- **End card:** final sunny hero wide shot, character waving, small in-scene signboard with social handles.

### Audio
Not analysed. The speech-to-text model was blocked in the analysis session, so the scripts were rebuilt from the
burned-in captions. The caption timing implies one continuous, fast narrator voice-over. Voice character, music
and sound effects still need a listen.

### Ground rules
- Copy the **formula**, never the content. Write new topics and new scripts. Don't reuse their wording,
  character design or "How Building Explained" branding.
- Pick topics with danger or fame built in. Avoid mundane utilities and "X-proof house" topics.
  Exception (section 4): an everyday thing works when its workings are hidden and it moves something the camera can follow
  (storm drain, 9.2M on @1hidden_by_design). Still avoid things whose workings are visible; the gas station flopped on both accounts.
- Avoid topics the account has already done: nipa hut, Amazon floating house, igloo, levee, nuclear bunker,
  nuclear plant, Chernobyl, White House, Kremlin, particle accelerator, armored truck, gas station, car wash,
  oil well, septic, drive-thru, slot machine, car engine, fire/volcano/earthquake-proof house, Halloween station.

## 2. Video ideas (approved 14 Nov 2026): four "Who wins?" matchups

**Approved by the user on 14 Nov 2026:** "I like p1-4 I don't like p5, update claude.md file remove the old video ideas replace with
these 4 for now". P1–P4 below are the whole list for now. P5 (a race car on the ceiling) was rejected. The ten ideas approved on
11 Nov 2026 were removed the same day; videos 05 and 06 came from that list ("idea #1", Aquarius, and "idea #9", Petra, in the
video log). The removed ideas and the full P5 are in the repo history at commit `92a077a`.

- **The brief** (7.3): ideas that keep people watching, make them comment and argue, and turn them into followers. Not only builds.
  The user suggested military, vehicles and engineering.
- **Why these topics:** tanks, warships and cars have large audiences that argue in the comments, and Shorts on these subjects reach
  millions (figures under each idea). Two cautions. These viewers punish wrong facts, so the fact-check matters more than before.
  And TikTok restricts weapon content (see "Platform rules" below), so the ideas stay with machines, history and what-ifs: no small
  arms, no how-to, no current wars, nobody shown getting hurt.
- **What the data says** (7.4): explainers in this format get almost no comments (0.2–0.3 per 1,000 views). What-ifs get two to
  four times more, and the most-commented videos put a choice to the viewer or touch something people already argue about. On the
  one account that posts both, what-ifs also have a median of 457K views against 35K for builds. So a good topic is not enough:
  each video needs a question the viewer can answer in two words.
- **The frame: every video is a matchup.** Two sides are on screen from the first frame, a scoreboard label counts through the
  video, and it ends on a verdict question. A vote is the easiest comment to write, and the scoreboard is a reason to stay.
- **Look:** Classic (3.4). The mascot is "you", on one of the two sides.
- **Rules for these ideas** (on top of sections 1 and 5):
  1. Hook: a claim that sounds wrong, with a number, inside the first 3–4 seconds. Both sides are in the first frame, and the labels
     must make the hook work with the sound off. Second person stays.
  2. First "But" by about 4 s. Seven or eight But/So beats, as before.
  3. Scoreboard: one or two labels that change through the video (SHELLS 42 → 0). Labels on the scene fit the 3.4 shot rule.
  4. One arena, one main view: the 3.4 shot rule applied to a matchup. The camera stays on the arena and every line changes
     something in it. To show how a machine works, slice it open from the same view.
  5. Ending: our loop line stays (4.3) and there is no spoken call to action (4.2). The verdict question is the last on-screen
     label and the caption.
  6. Answer the strongest objection with a short reply video (TikTok's reply-with-video). Likely objections are listed per idea.
  7. Never plant a mistake to bait corrections. What stays open is the verdict, not the facts.
- **Asked on 14 Nov 2026 and not answered yet: raise both at Gate A of the first matchup.**
  - P2, P3 and P4 open on a cold claim before the first "you", which breaks the "Open with If you…" line of the section 5 prompt.
    At Gate A, show the opening both ways and let the user pick.
  - Whether "Who wins?" becomes the running series, with the verdict question and "Top comment picks the next matchup" as the
    pinned comment. (@thewhatifstudio's captions say "You asked for it": requests are comments, and waiting for the result is a
    reason to follow.)
- **How we'll know:** our six videos have 9 comments on 34.7K views (0.26 per 1,000) and 1.3 followers per 1,000 views. Target
  for the first matchup: 1 comment and 3 followers per 1,000 views. Ask the user for average watch time and "watched full video"
  from TikTok's analytics for the old videos and the new one; we can't see those from outside.
- **Order I'd make them in:** P4 first (the widest audience and no weapons), then P1, P3, P2.
- **Chains are drafts:** ✔ was checked by web search on 14 Nov 2026; ⚠ still needs checking before Gate A. Rerun the saturation
  check on the title before Gate A (6.7).

**P1. One Modern Tank vs 100 Tiger Tanks** (a what-if: an M1A2 Abrams in 1944)
- Arena: a French field with hedgerows from a raised three-quarter view. Our sand-coloured tank sits on a ridge at the left with
  the mascot in the commander's hatch; grey Tigers fill the field. Labels: SHELLS 42 and TIGERS 100.
- Hook: "If you take one modern tank back to 1944, nothing on the battlefield can hurt you. But you only have 42 shells, and a
  hundred Tigers are coming." Alternates: "One modern tank. Forty-two shells. A hundred Tiger tanks. Who wins?"; "A modern tank
  could fight all of 1944 and still lose."
- Chain: their 88 mm guns kill anything in 1944 -> your layered front armour breaks their shells up ⚠ -> there are a hundred of
  them -> you fire first, from about 2.5 miles against their half mile, and the computer makes the first shot count ⚠ (both
  counters fall) -> night comes -> you see heat and they see nothing (THERMAL SIGHT) ⚠ -> they circle to your thinner sides ⚠ ->
  you never stop: you shoot on the move (STABILIZER) while they must halt to aim ⚠ -> one hit on a track and you are stuck
  (MOBILITY KILL) -> SHELLS 0, TIGERS 58: your machine guns only scratch them, and nobody in 1944 makes your ammunition -> your
  jet engine burns about two gallons a mile ✔ (500 gallons for about 265 road miles) -> it can drink their fuel (MULTI-FUEL) ⚠.
- Twist: the tank is not the weapon; the supply line is. Without its trucks a modern tank is out of the fight in hours.
- Ending: "Pick your shots, guard your tracks, and count to forty-two." Verdict: "42 SHELLS vs 100 TIGERS: WHO WINS?"
- Objections to answer: "just ram them", "the Tigers break down first", "what about their planes".
- Why it should work: who-would-win is what the tank audience comments on, and the shell count makes the answer truly open.
- Shorts: "Tanks Lore" 9.1M, "Modern Tank in The Napoleonic Wars?" 1.7M, "Tiger I vs Leopard 2A7" 968K. Closest: "20 WWII Tiger
  Tanks vs 1 Modern M1 Abrams: Who Would Win?" 215K. Long video "100 MAUS vs 1 ABRAMS" 2.7M.
- Checked ✔: 42 rounds, 500 gallons, 42 mph on roads and 25 off-road, 265-mile road range. Watch out: machines only, no flags or
  symbols on the Tigers, nobody shown hit.

**P2. The $100 Million Submarine That "Sank" a $4.5 Billion Aircraft Carrier** (HSwMS Gotland and USS Ronald Reagan, war games, 2005)
- Arena: a slice of ocean. The carrier and its escorts are on the surface, the small submarine is below, the seabed at the bottom.
  The mascot is at the periscope inside the sliced-open sub. Labels: $100M and $4.5B, then DISTANCE TO CARRIER counting down.
- Hook: "In a 2005 war game, a 100-million-dollar submarine sank a 4.5-billion-dollar aircraft carrier. Then the Navy rented the
  submarine." Alternate: "If you want to sink an aircraft carrier, you don't need a bigger ship. You need a quieter one."
- Chain: if you want to do it, you have to pass the bodyguards: destroyers, a cruiser, helicopters and a hunting submarine, all
  listening ⚠ -> a diesel sub must come up for air to run its engines, and radar spots the snorkel ⚠ -> bring your own air: liquid
  oxygen feeds a STIRLING ENGINE that charges the batteries underwater ✔, for about two weeks ⚠ -> engines are loud -> this one
  burns steadily with no explosions, and you can switch almost everything else off; a nuclear sub's reactor pumps never stop ⚠ ->
  aircraft sniff for your steel hull with magnetic detectors -> electromagnets cancel your magnetic field ⚠ -> you are slow, about
  5 knots ⚠ -> so you don't chase; you wait where the carrier must pass (AMBUSH) -> you slide under the escorts, raise the
  periscope and photograph the carrier, which in a war game is a kill ⚠.
- Twist: the US Navy leased the boat and its Swedish crew for a year, then for a second ✔, to learn how to find it.
- Ending: "Bring your own air, kill the noise, and wait." Verdict: "$100M SUB vs $4.5B CARRIER: ARE CARRIERS OBSOLETE?"
- Objections to answer: "war games tie the carrier's hands", "a carrier does 30 knots and the sub does 5", "nuclear subs are better".
- Why it should work: the cheap thing beats the expensive thing, nobody was hurt, and the verdict is a live argument among navy fans.
- Shorts: best 1M ("How a US aircraft carrier sunk by a Swedish submarine"), then 154K and 12K. Real Engineering's long video has
  10.1M. A proven story that is thin on Shorts.
- Checked ✔: the 2005 exercise, the simulated torpedo attack, the 2005–2007 lease with a Swedish crew. The prices are press
  figures (the carrier is also quoted at $6B). Always say "in a war game" in the same sentence as "sank".

**P3. The Pickup Trucks That Beat a Tank Army** (the Toyota War, Chad against Libya; Battle of Fada, 2 Jan 1987)
- Arena: a desert base from a raised three-quarter view. Tanks are dug into sand walls facing outward; white pickups circle with
  dust trails. The mascot drives the lead pickup. Labels: TANKS LOST 0 → 92 and PICKUPS LOST 0 → 3.
- Hook: "In 1987, an army in pickup trucks destroyed 92 tanks in a single battle. It lost three pickups." Alternate: "If your enemy
  has tanks and you have pickup trucks, you have one rule: never slow down."
- Chain: if you fight a tank from a pickup, one shell ends you -> a tank's turret takes about 20 seconds to turn a full circle ⚠, so
  at 60 mph you cross its sights faster than it can follow (SPEED IS ARMOUR) ⚠ -> your bullets bounce off -> bolt an anti-tank
  missile launcher in the bed: the French MILAN ✔ reaches about a mile and burns through the armour ⚠ -> minefields ring the base ->
  reportedly you cross them flat out, so the mines go off behind you ⚠ (verify or cut) -> there are far more tanks than trucks ->
  they are dug in and face one way; you come from every side at once, two pickups to a tank ✔ (SWARM) -> their jets -> shoulder-
  fired missiles ride in the trucks too ⚠.
- Twist: the war is named after the trucks, the Toyota War; about 400 of them came from France ✔.
- Ending: "Bolt on a missile, floor it, and never slow down." Verdict: "TANK OR TOYOTA?" (pinned: "Was it the trucks or the tactics?")
- Objections to answer: "the tank crews were badly trained", "try that against a modern tank", "the missiles did it, not the trucks".
- Why it should work: an underdog with a score nobody believes, and two tribes at once (truck owners and military fans).
- Shorts: 5.9M ("Why Toyota is the Market Leader of Modern War"), 1.8M, 683K ("This Pickup Truck Beat 300 Tanks").
- Checked ✔ (Wikipedia's Battle of Fada figures): 92 T-55 tanks and 33 other armoured vehicles destroyed, 3 pickups lost, 784
  Libyan and 18 Chadian dead. Watch out: a real battle with real dead. Count machines, show nobody hit, draw no badges.

**P4. 1959 Car vs 2009 Car: One Driver Walks Away** (the IIHS crash test of 9 Sep 2009)
- Arena: a crash-test hall. The two cars face each other on a track, both sliced open along the side so the cabins show. The
  mascot is "you" as the crash dummy, in his yellow hard hat. Labels: 1959, 2009, 40 MPH, then CABIN PUSHED IN: 62 cm and 7 cm.
- Hook: "One of these drivers walks away. The other doesn't. Pick your car." Then: "If you picked the old one, you picked nearly
  two tons of solid steel ⚠. But solid is the problem." Alternates: "They don't build them like they used to. Good."; "If you crash
  a 1959 car into a 2009 car, you'd bet on the old one. You'd lose."
- Chain: steel that doesn't bend stops the car in an instant, and you keep going at 40 mph until the steering wheel stops you ⚠ ->
  so the new car is built to wreck itself: the front folds like an accordion and stretches the stop (CRUMPLE ZONE) -> if the whole
  car folded it would fold onto you -> a cage of stronger steel around the seats holds its shape (SAFETY CAGE): the old cabin was
  pushed in 62 cm at the driver's feet, the new one 7 cm ✔ -> you still fly forward -> the belt locks and pulls tight ⚠ and an
  airbag fills in about 30 thousandths of a second ⚠ -> the steering column is a spear -> it collapses instead ⚠.
- Twist ("that's why"): a small crash "totals" a modern car because it is built to wreck itself for you. Per mile driven, far fewer
  Americans die on the road than in 1959 ⚠.
- Ending: "Fold the front, cage the middle, and catch the driver." Verdict: "STILL THINK THEY DON'T BUILD THEM LIKE THEY USED TO?"
- Objections to answer: "old cars were real steel", "that old car was rusty", "try it with an old pickup".
- Why it should work: everyone drives, everyone has heard the saying, and the answer starts a fight between generations. The
  opening makes the viewer choose a side before the first fact.
- Shorts: 2.8M ("Crash Test: Classic vs Modern"), 926K ("What Happens When a 1959 Chevy Hits a Modern Car?"), 326K: all real
  footage. The test videos have 12M and 10.9M. No animated "why" version found.
- Checked ✔: a 40 mph offset frontal test; the Bel Air's cabin collapsed and the Malibu's stayed intact; 62 cm against 7 cm at the
  footrest; rated Poor against Good; the Malibu's worst score was the left leg and foot. Watch out: the dummy takes the hit and
  nobody "dies" on screen; no car badges.

### 3.3 Step-by-step

**Step 1: Topic and script (free).**
- Pick the idea from section 2. Write the script with the formula and writing rules in section 1 and the script rules in the section 5
  prompt. Target ~75 s, ~260–280 words; never under 60 s.
- Write for the build (3.4 shot rule): every line must be something the viewer can see happen on the build. Comparisons, stats and
  the "that's why" are shown as labels on the build; cut any line that would need a picture of something else. At Gate A, list the
  lines that became labels and the lines that were cut.
- Run the saturation check on the title (`pipeline/youtube/yt_saturation.py`, 6.7) and report near-identical Shorts and the best views.
  Search the exact title, "how a [thing] becomes [place]" and "[place] how it was built": in video 06 only the second found the 27K
  near-identical build (3.4h).
- Fact-check every ⚠ claim with web search before locking. Remove anything unverifiable.
- Show the script. **GATE A: user approves the script.**

**Step 2: Beat sheet (free).**
- Split the script into beats, one sentence or one "But/So" unit each (usually 14–18 beats).
- For each beat, write: `beat_id | VO text | est. seconds (words ÷ 3.5) | shot type (problem / build / twist / aside / climax / payoff / cozy / end) | stage still used | camera move | label text (if any) | animate? (Lite: Y on hook, 1st build, twist, 2–3 key builds, climax, payoff, end)`.
- Define the **stage ladder**: an ordered list of how the structure looks after each fix (Stage 0 = bare ground → Stage N = finished build). Each build beat moves from stage k to k+1.
- **Every beat shows the build** (3.4 shot rule): the main view at the current stage, a close-up of the build at that stage, or the
  wall sliced open from the main view. Problems hit the build in the main view (the storm over it, the water pouring in). If the build
  itself has to move to a new setting, give that setting its own main view and continue the ladder there.

**Step 3: Cost quote. GATE B.**
- `balance`, then `get_cost:true` per model/duration combo, then the total plus reserve.
- Price the narration too: `creative_generate_speech` with `estimate_only:true` shows the ElevenLabs cost and any quota block. In video 05 one
  take of a 272-word script cost 1,368 ElevenLabs credits, the plan had 354 left, and the first try failed until the user upgraded.
- Present: tier, number of stills, number and length of clips, total credits, remaining balance after.
- **Wait for approval.** Nothing below this line runs without it.

**Step 4: Project and style lock.**
- `get_preferences`; if asked or preferred, `create_project` named after the video and reuse its `folder_id` on every generation.
- Write the **STYLE FORMULA** (80–100 words) once and paste it byte-identical into every image and video prompt. Base it on section 1's animation style. Default:
  > "3D CG render, real-time game-engine look, semi-realistic PBR textures on simple low-to-mid-poly models, soft natural daylight, soft shadows, mild depth of field, small diorama-like set, natural earthy palette, clean readable shapes, vertical 9:16 composition with the subject in the centre third, no text, no captions, no logos, no watermark."
- **Our own mascot** (never copy theirs): one stock-style rigged builder, e.g. a "stocky man in a yellow hard hat, navy work overalls, brown boots, simple face, no facial animation". Lock the description and reuse it verbatim.
- Generate in ONE `generate_image_batch`: (0) style key = a representative wide of the set at Stage 0 with the mascot, 9:16; (1) mascot full-body on a plain backdrop, 2:3. `jobs_wait`, then `show_generation_by_ids`. If off-style, regenerate once.

**Step 5: Stage stills (~1.25 cr each).**
- For each stage on the ladder, one 9:16 still of the SAME set from the SAME hero angle, passing the style key and mascot job ids as `medias` role `image_references` (order: location → character → props), plus the previous stage still so only the new part changes. Prompt pattern: `[STYLE FORMULA] Same scene and camera as reference. Now [exact change for this stage]. Everything else identical.`
- Extra stills are only versions of the current stage in the same view: a night or storm grade, the wall sliced open, the threat
  arriving. Make each by editing the current stage still ("Keep exactly the same camera position, zoom and framing as the reference
  image"; "Change only this: …"; 3.4f, 3.4g). A close-up of the build gets its own still only when a push-in on the stage still would
  be too soft to read (a gauge, a latch); make it by editing the stage still as well. Never make a still of something away from the
  build. Before Gate C, check that every still is the main view, a close-up of the build or the sliced-open wall, and that the build
  matches from stage to stage.
- Submit in batches of ≤12, `jobs_wait` (≤12 per call), one `show_generation_by_ids`. Regenerate drifted stills once. **GATE C: show the still ladder; user approves.**

**Step 6: Narration.**
- Preferred: ElevenLabs connector. `creative_list_voices`, pick a calm, confident male or female explainer voice (or the voice locked in 3.4), and generate the **whole script as ONE continuous take** (sounds like the source: fast, even, no pauses). Two takes come back by default; pick one.
- Fallback: Higgsfield `text2speech_v2` + `variant:"elevenlabs"` with the locked voice pair. Never mix voices within a video.
- Pace target: ~3.5–3.8 words/s. If the take is much slower, tighten the script. Never speed-shift audio more than 1.1×.
- Get word timestamps in the Higgsfield sandbox: download the audio with curl, then run faster-whisper (or `audio_to_captions.py --script <manifest> --max-words 1`) so the authored words supply the text and Whisper only supplies the timing. Derive each beat's real start and end. **These timings replace the estimates from Step 2.**

**Step 7: Pilot clips. GATE D.**
- Render 2 clips with the default video model (3.0; `kling4_0` since video 04): the hook beat and the first build beat.
- Build beat: `start_image` = stage k still, `end_image` = stage k+1 still. Prompt: `[STYLE FORMULA] [camera move, e.g. slow push-in]. [what physically happens, e.g. four bamboo legs slide out and lift the platform]. Motion starts on frame 1. The character only gestures and does not talk. No text, no captions.`
- Problem beat: `start_image` = current stage still (night or storm version if needed). Prompt describes the threat moving (rain falling, water rising, snake crawling).
- Close-ups happen inside the clip ("slow push-in toward the door"), and the next shot is back in the main view. The camera never
  travels away from the build.
- `duration` = the beat's measured length rounded up (min 4). Show both. If the look fails, fix the stills or prompt, not by re-rolling.

**Step 8: Remaining clips.**
- Batch the rest (Full: all beats; Lite: only beats flagged Y) in `generate_video_batch` chunks of ≤12, `jobs_wait`, then one `show_generation_by_ids`.
- Check each clip: right stage, no drift, no text artefacts, motion from frame 1. One corrected regeneration max per clip.

**Step 9: Assembly (Higgsfield sandbox, free).**
- Download all clips, stills and narration into one working folder in a single `sandbox_exec` (use `background:true` for long renders, `size:"xl"` only for the final encode).
- For each beat in order: trim its clip to the beat's measured length (from the Step 6 timestamps). For non-animated beats, hold the last frame of the clip before with a slow push-in (free, and it matches because it is the same view), or turn the current stage still into a clip with a slow zoompan push-in or pan of that length. Hard cuts only, no transitions.
- Lay the narration on top at full volume. Optional: a quiet ambient bed (rain, jungle, wind) only if the user asked or provided one; never add copyrighted music.
- Output 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264 + AAC. Keep this as the **clean master** (no text).

**Step 10: Labels and captions (sandbox, free).**
- **Labels:** for each beat that names a term or stat, draw the ALL-CAPS label (bold white sans, soft shadow) near the subject for that beat's duration. Dimension lines (dashed line + "10 FT") are drawn the same way.
- **Captions:** one word per cue, timed from the Whisper word timestamps, text from the authored script. Centred horizontally at ~52% of frame height. Title Case. Heavy rounded font (fetch Fredoka Bold from Google Fonts inside the sandbox; fall back to the preinstalled Montserrat ExtraBold). White fill, ~4 px black outline, soft drop shadow, ~64–72 px at 1080 wide.
- This centred, one-word look is a deliberate choice for this format. The bundled `subtitle_paper_burn.py` only places captions at the bottom, so render these with an ASS subtitle file through ffmpeg/libass. Use the bundled scripts for transcription only.
- Verify: duration matches the master within 0.2 s, every script word appears exactly once, and 3 spot frames (start, middle, end) show the right word. Inspect them with `image_paths`.
- **Fallback:** if the caption burn fails, deliver the clean master plus an `.srt` of one-word cues, and the user captions it in CapCut (one-word preset, centred).

**Step 11: End card and delivery.**
- Final beat: a sunny hero wide of the finished build, mascot waving, with a small in-scene signboard. Put the user's handle on the signboard as a label in post (ask for the handle once and save it in 3.4).
- Upload the final MP4 to Higgsfield storage (`media_upload` → PUT in the same `sandbox_exec` → `media_confirm`) and give the user the link.
- Report: final length, credits quoted vs spent (`transactions`), any beats that fell back to stills.
- Only if asked: `virality_predictor` (ask about cost first), then `tiktok_prepare_publish` with `mode:"UPLOAD_TO_DRAFT"` (the user always reviews and submits).

**Step 12: Post copy (free).**
- TikTok: the idea's title or a hook question with one emoji, then `#HowItWorks #Engineering #LearnOnTikTok #3DAnimation` and 1–2 topic
  tags (video 04's pattern, 3.4f). The `social-media-skills:caption-writer` and `hook-writer` skills can help with alternates.
- **YouTube Shorts gets its own copy:** follow the upload checklist in 6.4 (a title in the idea's form, `How a ___ Becomes ___` or
  `If You…`, with no hashtags or call to action; a written description; audience set to "not made for kids").

### 3.5 Things that will go wrong, and the fix
- **Structure drifts between stages:** always pass the previous stage still as a reference and say "everything else identical". Regenerate the still, not the clip.
- **Video model adds text or subtitles:** add "no text, no captions, no letters" to the prompt and regenerate once.
- **Clip starts frozen:** add "motion starts on frame 1" and regenerate once.

### 4.2 Why it works
1. **Universal, hidden, and moving.** Everyone sees a storm drain, a tap or a manhole; nobody sees what's underneath. Each topic moves
   something (water, sewage, power, trash) along a path the camera can follow, so the title can ask "where does it go?" or "how does it get here?"
2. **Water is the star.** The five best mature videos all have water as the payload or the enemy (drain, well and tower, under-river tunnel,
   underwater foundation, sewer). Translucent water in a cutaway is satisfying to watch, and it hands the script its problems (floods, leaks,
   seepage, backflow).
3. **This refines our "avoid mundane utilities" rule.** Mundane works when the workings are hidden (storm drain, 9.2M). It still flops when
   the workings are already visible: the gas station flopped on both accounts (37K here, 41K on HBE).
4. **Titles name one concrete mystery the viewer owns:** "your street", "your house"; a paradox ("dig under a river without letting the
   water in"); or a transformation ("turn a garbage dump into a park"). The flop had the vaguest title ("...But How Does It Work?").
5. **Fast first problem.** The first "But" lands at 2.5–3.5 s on the top videos and at 4.6–6.5 s on the weaker ones.
6. **The top three end on a loop.** All three close on a three-beat imperative that flows back into the hook, with no "Congratulations" and
   no CTA: "...Bury the pipes, pave the road, and let it rain." → "If you cut a hole in your street, the rain has somewhere to go."
   So far every video that ends on "Congratulations" or the creator CTA has fewer views than these three (small sample; the landfill
   video has the CTA and is climbing fast).
7. **One continuous camera.** 8 of 9 videos have 0–2 hard cuts in ~75 s. The gas station had 27 cuts and close-up montages, and it flopped.
8. **Length.** The three best run 74–76 s; both 88 s videos underperformed. All run over 60 s (TikTok's Creator Rewards only pays on
   videos over a minute, which may be why).
9. **One small human joke per video:** "somebody's missing tennis ball", "your street becomes a swimming pool", "your drain becomes a
   fountain", "stroll over the same trash they left at the curb".
10. **A "that's why" viewers can check on their next walk:** "Those are the manholes you drive over every day." "That's why the handle
    clicks when your tank is full."

### 4.3 Script formula as they use it
```
HOOK (0-3s)  If you [naive action], [it sort of works].
             "If you cut a hole in your street, the rain has somewhere to go."
             (or a question: "You flush your toilet, and everything disappears. But where does it actually go?")
LOOP x7-8    But [problem]. So [fix]. (This is a [TERM].)        first "But" by ~3 s
THAT'S WHY   "Those are the manholes you drive over every day."
END          three-beat imperative that loops into the hook: "Bury the pipes, pave the road, and let it rain."
             (weaker variant: "Congratulations, you've built a [X]." + "If you wanna make videos like this, check out the link in my bio.")
```
- 235–326 words in 64–88 s, 3.4–4.0 words/s (top three: 269–302 words in ~75 s).
- Fixes are often bare imperatives ("So line the hole with compacted clay...", "Push a giant steel shield into the ground").
- Real terms are named only 0–4 times per video ("This is a catch basin."), and never shown as on-screen labels.
- Stakes are everyday disasters (flooding, sewage in the yard, collapse, fire, contamination, blackout), not the HBE "deadliest X" twist.

### 4.4 Animation style
- **Look:** bright, clean, photoreal-leaning 3D render: soft sunny daylight, warm tones, soft shadows, mild depth of field, clean materials
  (concrete, grey PVC, steel, terracotta roof tiles) and translucent cyan water. Almost certainly AI image-to-video, not hand animation
  (two long videos a day, morphing details, and they sell a "how I make these" guide). The landfill, roof and gas videos switch to an
  overcast, drone-documentary look with real-looking workers.
- **The cutaway is the signature:** the ground is sliced like a cake to show soil layers (topsoil, gravel, sand, clay, a blue groundwater
  band) with the pipes, chambers and tanks inside. Machines and fittings get see-through housings (check valve, fuel nozzle, transformer coils).
- **The world:** the same idyllic suburb every time (cream houses, terracotta roofs, blue shutters, lawns, a curving road, a creek), with a
  yellow excavator, a blue drill truck, and white or blue sedans.
- **No mascot.** "You" is the viewer. People appear only as hands at a tap, a woman flipping a light switch, small figures walking, or workers.
- **Camera:** one continuous, slowly moving camera. Transitions are fly-throughs that follow the flow: down the grate into the pipe, up the
  well to the kitchen tap, along the wires from the plant to the house. The camera path is the story.
- **Build moments:** each fix appears on the same cutaway set (pipe slides in, chamber grows, tank fills). This is our stage-ladder method.
- **Explainer devices:** small flat white arrows for flow and force, a white highlight circle, and a final labelled cutaway "summary
  diagram" (landfill: Cap, Gas Wells, Subsurface Waste, Bottom Liner System).
- **Captions:** one word at a time at ~72–78% of frame height (just above TikTok's caption area). Video 1 used lowercase white rounded sans
  with a soft shadow; from video 2 on, ALL CAPS heavy sans, white with a black stroke and drop shadow. No other on-screen text.
- **Covers:** a mid-video frame showing the most satisfying cutaway (the catch basin with grit, leaves and the tennis ball). No headline text.
- **End overlays:** an animated TikTok "FOLLOW" button with a cursor click, or a red YouTube "SUBSCRIBE 🔔" button, in the last ~3 s
  (they probably cross-post to YouTube Shorts).
- **Format:** 9:16, 30 fps, uploaded at 1080×1920.

### 4.5 Audio (measured, not listened to)
- The same narrator in all nine: fast, even and lively, almost certainly an AI voice. Median pitch ~150 Hz with a wide 118–239 Hz range,
  which suggests a male voice with lively intonation.
- A quiet, low, non-melodic stereo bed (ambient or cinematic pad) under the mono voice: pauses sit only 12–14 dB below speech. Integrated
  loudness about −23 LUFS. The gas station had a steadier, more tonal bed.

### 4.6 Topics @1hidden_by_design has already done (avoid)
Storm drain, water well and water tower, road tunnel under a river, landfill into a park, bridge foundation in a river (cofferdam), sewer
and wastewater treatment, power grid, gas station, roof.

### 6.9 What the views are worth (11 Nov 2026)
The user asked whether $3,000 a month is realistic from faceless AI Shorts "once I'm monetized". Answer given: not from Shorts ads
alone (that is the top channel of this format, every month); realistic only as a mix led by TikTok Creator Rewards and longer
videos, at the stage where one or two videos a month reach several million views. The figures below are creator-blog and
calculator numbers from web-search summaries, not the platforms' own pages: **re-check before quoting.**
- **Shorts pay:** usually quoted at $0.03–0.07 per 1,000 views; mostly-US audiences more (reports of $0.10–0.30). YouTube pools the
  Shorts feed's ad money per country and splits it by engaged views (the creator keeps 45% of their share, per secondary sources).
  Studio's Shorts RPM is per 1,000 engaged views, which run below the public view counter.
- **$3,000 a month from Shorts ads** = about 43–100M views a month (60M at $0.05). Scale check with the 6.3 snapshot: You Made It's
  nine best Shorts total ~95M views (about $2.9–6.7K) and You Built It's eight best ~28M (about $0.8–1.9K). The Partner Program's
  entry pace, 10M views per 90 days, is worth about $100–230 a month.
- **TikTok Creator Rewards:** 10K followers, 100K views in the last 30 days, age 18+, an eligible country, videos of 60 s or more,
  and only posts made after joining earn. Quoted at about $0.40–1.00 per 1,000 qualified views (some US figures up to $2), roughly
  ten times Shorts, so $3,000 = about 3–7.5M qualified views a month. Our ~75 s videos already qualify on length. **The account
  is US-based** (the user confirmed on 13 Nov 2026), and the US is an eligible country. TikTok's support page gives the same thresholds
  and adds that the account must be a personal one, not a Business account (search summary, 13 Nov 2026).
- **Long videos on YouTube:** quoted at $1–10 per 1,000 views, so $3,000 = about 0.3–3M views a month.
- **Cost side:** third-party pricing pages put Higgsfield at about $5 per 100 top-up credits (Plus $49–59 a month for 1,000–1,200
  credits; not checked against the user's own plan), so a Lite video costs roughly $5–7 and needs about 80–190K Shorts views to pay
  for itself from Shorts ads alone.
- **Reading the sources:** www.tubefilter.com, ppc.land and searchenginejournal.com are outside the Default environment's network
  policy (WebFetch can't resolve them); WebSearch summaries were used instead.
