# Short-Form 3D Build Explainers: Project Memory

This project makes short vertical 3D-animated explainer videos in the style of the TikTok account
@howbuildingexplained ("How Building Explained By Zach"). Read this file before writing any script,
storyboard or animation brief.

Full visual reference (frames, beat sheet, both reconstructed scripts):
https://claude.ai/artifact/MPEu5jqcnUDLSsz3fiBc8N (private "How Building Explained Style Bible" artifact; open it with the Artifact tool's read action)

## 1. What we learned from the source video

**Source:** "How A Branch Of Bamboo Can Turn Into A Nipa Hut in the jungle"
(https://www.tiktok.com/@howbuildingexplained/video/7703769950413933854). 66.6 s, 7.2M views,
731K likes, 18.4K shares. It was cross-checked against the account's #2 video ("How A Hole In Your Yard Can Become A Levee",
100 s, 1.9M views). Both follow the identical formula.

### The niche
- 60–110 s fully 3D-animated vertical explainers. A humble starting thing becomes an impressive structure,
  one problem and one fix at a time, narrated in second person ("you").
- Title formula: `How [humble thing] Can Become [impressive / famous / survival structure] (in the [setting])`
  plus `#engineering #HowItWorks #Science #LearnOnTikTok` and 1–2 topic tags.
- What wins on the account: **primitive survival builds** (jungle nipa hut 7.2M, Amazon floating house 769K)
  and **famous or high-stakes structures** (levee 1.9M, White House 1.6M, nuclear bunker 1.4M, Kremlin 1.0M,
  nuclear plant 976K, particle accelerator 900K).
- What flops: mundane utilities (car wash 9.5K, slot machine 10K, septic 25K, drive-thru 30K, oil well 41K) and
  the "X-proof house" series (fireproof 131K, volcano 95K, earthquake 79K). The igloo also did poorly (38K).
- The hit is one of the shortest videos. Tight scripts with more danger per second do better.

### Script formula
```
HOOK (0-3s)  If [you're in situation], [do a naive thing] and you have a [crude version].
LOOP x6-8    But [specific problem].  ->  So you [concrete fix].  ->  (This is / These are [REAL TERM].)
TWIST        But the deadliest [X] here isn't [obvious thing]. It's [surprise]. + one shock stat
ASIDE        And [fun fact]  /  That's why [thing viewers have seen in real life].
CLIMAX       But then [worst-case disaster] hits. [Everything around you is destroyed.]
PAYOFF       But yours [stands / stays] [safe] while [disaster happens right next to you].
COZY         (You) add [prop 1], [prop 2] and [prop 3].
SIGN-OFF     Congratulations, you have built a [X].      <- always this exact line
```

### Writing rules
- Second person, present tense. The viewer is always the builder ("you lift", "you drive", "you raise").
- One action or one problem per sentence. Short, mostly one-syllable words, no filler, no hedging.
- Every "But" escalates: nuisance -> weather -> hidden killer -> night threat -> catastrophe.
- After each fix, name the real-world term ("These are stilts." "This is thatch." "This is a rat guard.").
  The term also appears as an ALL-CAPS on-screen label.
- Use everyday and body-scale comparisons: "to your shoulders", "as high as a basketball hoop",
  "knee-high by morning", "hard as rock".
- At least one shock stat ("kills more people than snakes, sharks and lions combined", "84% fewer").
  **Fact-check every number before production.**
- One "that's why" tie-in ("That's why levees on the news are wrapped in plastic.").
- Pace: about 250 words per 66 s (~3.8 words/s, ~225 wpm). Long versions run ~100 s and ~350 words.

### Animation style
- **Look:** full 3D CG with a real-time, game-engine feel. Semi-realistic textures on simple models, soft shadows,
  mild depth of field. Not photoreal, not cartoon-shaded.
- **Set:** one small diorama (jungle clearing, suburban lawn) that stays the same location while the structure
  evolves on it.
- **Character:** one generic stock-rigged man (olive-green jumpsuit, no facial animation) for scale and
  simple poses: lying, standing, arms over head, waving. Machinery (excavator, backhoe, roller) can replace him.
- **Mood through grading:** daylight for fixes, dark desaturated for night threats, blue-grey for storms, sunny
  for the ending.
- **Camera:** about one shot per sentence (2–5 s), hard cuts on "But" and "So", slow push-ins and orbits within shots,
  macro close-ups of key details and then a pull back to the wide shot.
- **Build moments:** parts appear, slide or grow into place exactly when the narrator names them.
- **Problems** are shown with environmental effects: rain particles, a rising water plane, critter models, swarm particles,
  darkness.
- **Explainer devices** for invisible mechanisms: cross-section cutaways, magnifying-glass macro, x-ray pipes, blue water
  particles, red and blue force arrows, a yellow highlight ring, dashed measurement lines ("10 FT").
- **Captions:** ONE word at a time, synced to the voice, centred at ~52% frame height. Title Case, heavy rounded sans
  (Fredoka-like), white fill, thin black outline, soft drop shadow.
- **Labels:** ALL CAPS bold white sans with shadow, placed by the object, for the term or stat
  (STILTS, THATCH, DEADLIEST ANIMAL, 84% FEWER, RAT GUARD).
- **Format:** 9:16, 30 fps, 720×1280 or 1080×1920, 60–110 s.
- **End card:** final sunny hero wide shot, character waving, small in-scene signboard with social handles.

### Audio
Not analysed. The speech-to-text model was blocked in the analysis session, so the scripts were rebuilt from the
burned-in captions. The caption timing implies one continuous, fast narrator voice-over. Voice character, music
and sound effects still need a listen.

### Ground rules
- Copy the **formula**, never the content. Write new topics and new scripts. Don't reuse their wording,
  character design or "How Building Explained" branding.
- Pick topics with danger or fame built in. Avoid mundane utilities and "X-proof house" topics.
- Avoid topics the account has already done: nipa hut, Amazon floating house, igloo, levee, nuclear bunker,
  nuclear plant, Chernobyl, White House, Kremlin, particle accelerator, armored truck, gas station, car wash,
  oil well, septic, drive-thru, slot machine, car engine, fire/volcano/earthquake-proof house, Halloween station.

## 2. Video ideas (15)

Each idea lists the hero set, the hook, the escalation chain (problem -> fix -> LABEL), the twist, and the
climax and payoff. Facts marked ⚠ must be verified before scripting.

### Survival / primitive builds (closest to the 7.2M hit)

**1. How Mud Can Become A House In The Desert** — ✅ USED: video 01 (7 Nov 2026)
- Set: rocky desert flat, one cactus, dry riverbed.
- Hook: You stack rocks in a circle and you have a wall.
- Chain: rocks bake like a frying pan -> mud, straw and water pressed into bricks (ADOBE) -> wet bricks crumble ->
  sun-dried for weeks -> heat passes through thin walls -> walls two feet thick (THERMAL MASS) -> flat roof cooks you
  -> beams, branches and packed mud -> rain pools and melts the roof -> slight tilt and a clay spout (CANALE) ->
  whitewash with lime to reflect the sun.
- Twist: the desert's real killer at night is cold, not heat. The same thick walls give the day's heat back after dark.
- Climax/payoff: flash flood down the dry riverbed. The house was built on a rise and stays dry.
- Cozy: clay oven, small deep windows, wooden door.

**2. How A Fallen Tree Can Become A Canoe** — ✅ USED: video 02 (7 Nov 2026). As made: jungle river mouth that opens to the sea;
  storm waves instead of rapids (an outrigger float would snag in rapids); script in `pipeline/02-script.txt`.
- Set: tropical riverbank, one huge fallen log.
- Hook: You lie on a log and float, and you have a boat.
- Chain: log rolls and dumps you -> hollow it with fire and scraping (DUGOUT) -> water pours over the low sides ->
  carve a raised bow so it cuts the waves -> it still tips -> lash a second float on poles beside it (OUTRIGGER) ->
  hands as paddles are too slow -> carve paddles -> sun splits the wood -> keep it wet and oiled.
- Twist: the deadliest thing on the river isn't the crocodile. It's capsizing in the current. ⚠ need a solid stat.
- Climax/payoff: rapids. The outrigger keeps you upright.
- Cozy: woven sail, fishing spear, gourd of water.

**3. How A Cave Can Become A Home In The Mountains**
- Set: mountain cliff face, pine forest, snow line above.
- Hook: You walk into a cave out of the rain and you have a roof.
- Chain: cave is freezing -> fire at the back -> smoke fills the cave -> fire moved near the mouth, and a hole chipped up
  for the smoke (FLUE) -> drips everywhere -> raised log floor and a hide awning -> wind howls in -> stone wall across
  the entrance with a small door -> loose rock overhead -> tap the ceiling and pry down loose slabs (SCALING).
- Twist: the deadliest thing in the cave isn't the bear. It's the smoke. Carbon monoxide has no smell. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: blizzard and avalanche. The rock above protects you while snow buries the valley.
- Cozy: fur bed, drying rack of fish, firewood stack.

**4. How Logs Can Become A Cabin In Alaska**
- Set: snowy spruce clearing, frozen lake.
- Hook: You lean logs against a tree and you have a shelter.
- Chain: wind blows through -> stack logs and notch the corners so they lock (SADDLE NOTCH) -> gaps whistle -> stuff them
  with moss and mud (CHINKING) -> snow piles up and the flat roof sags -> steep roof so snow slides off -> open fire smokes
  you out -> iron wood stove with a chimney -> bears smell your food -> a tiny food hut on tall stilts (CACHE).
- Twist: the deadliest thing in Alaska isn't the bear, it's the cold. Wet clothes steal heat far faster than dry air. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: −40° night. Everything freezes but your cabin glows.
- Cozy: snowshoes on the wall, kettle on the stove, sled dog.

**5. How A Hole In The Sand Can Become An Underground Town**
(based on Coober Pedy, Australia)
- Set: red outback desert, opal mine spoil heaps.
- Hook: You dig a hole to hide from the sun and you have a shade.
- Chain: 50°C at noon ⚠ -> dig sideways into the hill (DUGOUT) -> stays about 24°C all year ⚠ -> air goes stale ->
  vertical shaft to the surface (VENT SHAFT) -> darkness -> skylight shaft -> sand walls collapse -> carve into
  sandstone that holds itself up -> neighbours dig next to you -> a whole town underground with a church and a hotel.
- Twist: people here also dig for opal, so digging a new room can pay for itself. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: dust storm and record heatwave on the surface. Underground you're cool.
- Cozy: carved shelves, pool table, opal glinting in the wall.

**6. How A Hole In The Ground Can Become A Well**
- Set: dry village clearing, cracked earth.
- Hook: You dig a hole until it's muddy and you have water.
- Chain: water is brown -> dig past the water table into gravel -> walls cave in -> line with stone rings (CASING) ->
  rain washes dirt and animal waste in -> raised wall and sloped concrete ring (APRON) -> latrine nearby leaks in ->
  keep it far away, uphill or not at all ⚠ distance -> buckets are heavy -> crank and rope (WINDLASS) -> lid.
- Twist: the deadliest animal here isn't the lion, it's the germ in dirty water. ⚠ WHO stat on unsafe water deaths.
- Climax/payoff: drought. Shallow holes dry up but yours goes below the water table.
- Cozy: bucket, clay jars, shade tree.

### Famous / high-stakes structures

**7. How A Pile Of Rocks Can Become A Castle**
- Set: grassy hill above a river.
- Hook: You pile rocks in a ring and you have a wall.
- Chain: enemies climb it -> taller wall with a walkway (CURTAIN WALL) -> they ram the gate -> drop-down iron grid
  (PORTCULLIS) -> they shoot your archers -> narrow slots (ARROW SLITS) -> they tunnel under the wall -> water ditch
  (MOAT) -> square towers crack at the corners -> round towers -> attackers in the gatehouse -> holes in the ceiling
  (MURDER HOLES).
- Twist: the deadliest weapon in a siege isn't the catapult. It's hunger and disease, so you add a well and a granary. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: army surrounds you for months. Walls hold, water flows.
- Cozy: banners, a cat in the granary, torch sconces.

**8. How A Bucket Of Stone Can Become A Lighthouse**
- Set: lone rock reef in a stormy sea.
- Hook: You light a fire on a rock and ships can see you.
- Chain: waves wash the fire away -> stone tower -> waves knock the stones loose -> interlocking dovetailed granite
  (DOVETAIL) -> straight tower still topples -> wide curved base like a tree trunk ⚠ Smeaton's Eddystone -> fire is too
  dim -> glass rings that bend the light into a beam (FRESNEL LENS) -> ships can't tell lighthouses apart -> each one
  flashes its own pattern -> fog hides the light -> fog horn.
- Twist: one lens can throw light 20+ miles. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: giant storm wave hits the top. The tower stands, and the ship misses the reef.
- Cozy: keeper's lamp, stairs, logbook, seagull.

**9. How A Wooden Box Can Become A Bank Vault**
- Set: small-town bank interior, night.
- Hook: You lock your money in a wooden box and you have a safe.
- Chain: robber smashes it -> steel walls -> they drill the lock -> hardened plate in front of the lock -> they cut
  through steel -> concrete with steel mesh inside (REBAR) -> they come at night -> lock that won't open until morning
  even with the code (TIME LOCK) -> they force the lock -> a hidden backup bolt fires (RELOCKER) -> they dig in from
  next door -> sensors in the walls.
- Twist: most bank robberies never touch the vault. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: heist crew with thermal lance at night. Alarm, vault holds.
- Cozy: safe-deposit boxes, round door, gold bars.

**10. How A Ditch Can Become The Panama Canal**
- Set: jungle isthmus diorama with ocean on both sides.
- Hook: You dig a ditch from one ocean to the other and you have a shortcut.
- Chain: mountains in the way -> dig through (CULEBRA CUT) -> landslides refill it -> wider, gentler slopes -> too much
  rock to dig to sea level -> dam a river into a giant lake high above the sea (GATUN LAKE) -> ships can't climb ->
  water elevators (LOCKS) -> lifting 85 feet ⚠.
- Twist: the deadliest thing on the canal wasn't the landslides. It was the mosquito. ⚠ ~22,000 deaths during the French
  attempt; tie back to mosquitoes from the nipa hut hit.
- Climax/payoff: the first giant ship crosses continents in a day.
- Cozy: tugboat, mule locomotives, flags.

**11. How A Stack Of Bricks Can Become A Pyramid**
- Set: desert plateau with the Nile in the distance.
- Hook: You stack blocks in a square and you have a pyramid.
- Chain: blocks too heavy to lift -> drag them on wooden sleds -> sleds dig into the sand -> pour water in front so the sand
  firms up ⚠ the 2014 wet-sand study -> pile is getting too high -> a ramp up the side (RAMP) -> rough steps look ugly ->
  polished white casing stones -> king's room crushed by the weight above -> stacked empty rooms above it
  (RELIEVING CHAMBERS) -> robbers -> granite plug blocks.
- Twist: the workers weren't slaves. They were paid crews with their own village and bakeries. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: thousands of years later, it's still standing.
- Cozy: sphinx, boats on the Nile, sunset glint off the casing.

**12. How A Pile Of Dirt Can Become The Hoover Dam**
- Set: deep red-rock canyon with a river.
- Hook: You pile dirt across a river and you have a dam.
- Chain: river washes it away -> blast tunnels to send the river around (DIVERSION TUNNELS) -> dirt is too weak -> concrete
  -> concrete gets hot as it hardens and would take 100+ years to cool ⚠ -> pipes of cold water through it (COOLING PIPES)
  -> pour it in blocks -> curved like a bow so the water presses it into the canyon walls (ARCH-GRAVITY) -> lake overflows
  -> side channels (SPILLWAYS) -> falling water spins turbines.
- Twist: a fact about the workers or the sheer amount of concrete. ⚠ pick one verified stat.
- Climax/payoff: record flood. The lake rises and the spillways roar, but the dam holds.
- Cozy: lit power station, observation deck, tiny cars on top.

**13. How A Hole In A Mountain Can Become A Tunnel Through The Alps**
- Set: snowy alpine valley cut away to show the mountain's insides.
- Hook: You dig into a mountain and you have a cave.
- Chain: it's too slow by hand -> giant spinning drill (TUNNEL BORING MACHINE) -> ceiling crumbles -> spray concrete
  (SHOTCRETE) and steel bolts into the rock (ROCK BOLTS) -> water floods in -> waterproof liner and drains -> deep inside
  the rock is hot ⚠ ~45°C at Gotthard -> chillers -> a train fire would trap everyone -> two tubes joined by escape
  passages (CROSS PASSAGES).
- Twist: the tunnel is longer than the mountain is tall. ⚠ use the Gotthard Base Tunnel at 57 km.
- Climax/payoff: blizzard closes every mountain pass. Your train runs under it all.
- Cozy: train headlights, glowing exit signs, cows at the portal.

### Extreme environments

**14. How A Tent Can Become A Research Station In Antarctica**
- Set: flat white ice shelf, endless horizon.
- Hook: You pitch a tent on the ice and you have a base.
- Chain: wind shreds it -> rounded pods that wind slides around -> heat melts the ice under you and you sink -> legs lift it
  off the ice -> snow buries it every year -> legs that jack themselves up (HYDRAULIC LEGS) -> the ice shelf cracks ->
  skis on the legs so you can tow the whole base inland ⚠ Halley VI -> six months of darkness -> one bright social pod.
- Twist: the deadliest thing here isn't the cold, it's fire. The air is dry and all the water is frozen. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: polar winter storm. The base sits above the snow, warm and lit.
- Cozy: penguins outside, greenhouse pod, flag.

**15. How A Tin Can Can Become A Space Station**
- Set: Earth curve below, black space, sun glare.
- Hook: You seal yourself in a can and launch it, and you have a spaceship.
- Chain: air leaks out -> airtight walls -> space dust at bullet speed -> thin outer bumper that shatters it first
  (WHIPPLE SHIELD) -> sun side bakes and shadow side freezes -> folding panels that dump heat (RADIATORS) -> no
  power -> wings of solar panels -> water runs out -> machines that recycle your sweat and urine into drinking water ⚠ ->
  bones weaken -> treadmill with bungee straps.
- Twist: the deadliest thing up here isn't the vacuum. It's fire. ⚠
- Climax/payoff: solar storm. You shelter in the most shielded module while the station glows past the aurora.
- Cozy: cupola window, floating snacks, a plant growing in a pouch.

### 3.2 Credit rules (hard)
1. Script, beat sheet, prompts and the cost quote are free. Do them all first.
2. Before Gate B, add up the exact plan with `get_cost:true` (one call per distinct model/duration combo), multiply out, add 15% reserve, show the total, and wait for approval.
3. Images before video. Fix look, consistency and drift in stills (1.25 cr), never by re-rolling clips (8–20 cr).
4. Pilot first: render only 2 clips (the hook and one build beat) and show them before the batch.
5. `count: 1` always. No variants. Regenerate a failed or bad clip **once**, with a corrected prompt. If it fails again, fall back to the still with ffmpeg motion and say so.
6. Clip `duration` = beat length rounded up to whole seconds (min 4). Never generate 10 s for a 4 s beat.
7. Never use `use_unlim` unless the user asks. Never auto-retry after a transport timeout; check the job id first.
8. After the run, call `transactions` and report the actual spend against the quote.

### 3.3 Step-by-step

**Step 1: Topic and script (free).**
- Pick the idea from section 2. Write the script with the formula and writing rules in section 1. Target length: 60–70 s, ~230–260 words.
- Fact-check every ⚠ claim with web search before locking. Remove anything unverifiable.
- Show the script. **GATE A: user approves the script.**

**Step 2: Beat sheet (free).**
- Split the script into beats, one sentence or one "But/So" unit each (usually 14–18 beats).
- For each beat, write: `beat_id | VO text | est. seconds (words ÷ 3.5) | shot type (problem / build / twist / aside / climax / payoff / cozy / end) | stage still used | camera move | label text (if any) | animate? (Lite: Y on hook, 1st build, twist, 2–3 key builds, climax, payoff, end)`.
- Define the **stage ladder**: an ordered list of how the structure looks after each fix (Stage 0 = bare ground → Stage N = finished build). Each build beat moves from stage k to k+1.

**Step 3: Cost quote. GATE B.**
- `balance`, then `get_cost:true` per model/duration combo, then the total plus reserve.
- Present: tier, number of stills, number and length of clips, total credits, remaining balance after.
- **Wait for approval.** Nothing below this line runs without it.

**Step 4: Project and style lock.**
- `get_preferences`; if asked or preferred, `create_project` named after the video and reuse its `folder_id` on every generation.
- Write the **STYLE FORMULA** (80–100 words) once and paste it byte-identical into every image and video prompt. Base it on section 1's animation style. Default:
  > "3D CG render, real-time game-engine look, semi-realistic PBR textures on simple low-to-mid-poly models, soft natural daylight, soft shadows, mild depth of field, small diorama-like set, natural earthy palette, clean readable shapes, vertical 9:16 composition with the subject in the centre third, no text, no captions, no logos, no watermark."
- **Our own mascot** (never copy theirs): one stock-style rigged builder, e.g. a "stocky man in a yellow hard hat, navy work overalls, brown boots, simple face, no facial animation". Lock the description and reuse it verbatim.
- Generate in ONE `generate_image_batch`: (0) style key = a representative wide of the set at Stage 0 with the mascot, 9:16; (1) mascot full-body on a plain backdrop, 2:3. `jobs_wait`, then `show_generation_by_ids`. If off-style, regenerate once.

**Step 5: Stage stills (~1.25 cr each).**
- For each stage on the ladder, one 9:16 still of the SAME set from the SAME hero angle, passing the style key and mascot job ids as `medias` role `image_references` (order: location → character → props), plus the previous stage still so only the new part changes. Prompt pattern: `[STYLE FORMULA] Same scene and camera as reference. Now [exact change for this stage]. Everything else identical.`
- Add extra stills only where a beat needs a different shot: night or storm grade, macro close-up, cutaway, critter or threat.
- Submit in batches of ≤12, `jobs_wait` (≤12 per call), one `show_generation_by_ids`. Regenerate drifted stills once. **GATE C: show the still ladder; user approves.**

**Step 6: Narration.**
- Preferred: ElevenLabs connector. `creative_list_voices`, pick a calm, confident male or female explainer voice (or the voice locked in 3.4), and generate the **whole script as ONE continuous take** (sounds like the source: fast, even, no pauses). Two takes come back by default; pick one.
- Fallback: Higgsfield `text2speech_v2` + `variant:"elevenlabs"` with the locked voice pair. Never mix voices within a video.
- Pace target: ~3.5–3.8 words/s. If the take is much slower, tighten the script. Never speed-shift audio more than 1.1×.
- Get word timestamps in the Higgsfield sandbox: download the audio with curl, then run faster-whisper (or `audio_to_captions.py --script <manifest> --max-words 1`) so the authored words supply the text and Whisper only supplies the timing. Derive each beat's real start and end. **These timings replace the estimates from Step 2.**

**Step 7: Pilot clips. GATE D.**
- Render 2 clips with `minimax_h3`: the hook beat and the first build beat.
- Build beat: `start_image` = stage k still, `end_image` = stage k+1 still. Prompt: `[STYLE FORMULA] [camera move, e.g. slow push-in]. [what physically happens, e.g. four bamboo legs slide out and lift the platform]. Motion starts on frame 1. The character only gestures and does not talk. No text, no captions.`
- Problem beat: `start_image` = current stage still (night or storm version if needed). Prompt describes the threat moving (rain falling, water rising, snake crawling).
- `duration` = the beat's measured length rounded up (min 4). Show both. If the look fails, fix the stills or prompt, not by re-rolling.

**Step 8: Remaining clips.**
- Batch the rest (Full: all beats; Lite: only beats flagged Y) in `generate_video_batch` chunks of ≤12, `jobs_wait`, then one `show_generation_by_ids`.
- Check each clip: right stage, no drift, no text artefacts, motion from frame 1. One corrected regeneration max per clip.

**Step 9: Assembly (Higgsfield sandbox, free).**
- Download all clips, stills and narration into one working folder in a single `sandbox_exec` (use `background:true` for long renders, `size:"xl"` only for the final encode).
- For each beat in order: trim its clip to the beat's measured length (from the Step 6 timestamps). For non-animated (Lite) beats, turn the still into a clip with a slow zoompan push-in or pan of that length. Hard cuts only, no transitions.
- Lay the narration on top at full volume. Optional: a quiet ambient bed (rain, jungle, wind) only if the user asked or provided one; never add copyrighted music.
- Output 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264 + AAC. Keep this as the **clean master** (no text).

**Step 10: Labels and captions (sandbox, free).**
- **Labels:** for each beat that names a term or stat, draw the ALL-CAPS label (bold white sans, soft shadow) near the subject for that beat's duration. Dimension lines (dashed line + "10 FT") are drawn the same way.
- **Captions:** one word per cue, timed from the Whisper word timestamps, text from the authored script. Centred horizontally at ~52% of frame height. Title Case. Heavy rounded font (fetch Fredoka Bold from Google Fonts inside the sandbox; fall back to the preinstalled Montserrat ExtraBold). White fill, ~4 px black outline, soft drop shadow, ~64–72 px at 1080 wide.
- This centred, one-word look is a deliberate choice for this format. The bundled `subtitle_paper_burn.py` only places captions at the bottom, so render these with an ASS subtitle file through ffmpeg/libass. Use the bundled scripts for transcription only.
- Verify: duration matches the master within 0.2 s, every script word appears exactly once, and 3 spot frames (start, middle, end) show the right word. Inspect them with `image_paths`.
- **Fallback:** if the caption burn fails, deliver the clean master plus an `.srt` of one-word cues, and the user captions it in CapCut (one-word preset, centred).

**Step 11: End card and delivery.**

### 3.5 Things that will go wrong, and the fix
- **Structure drifts between stages:** always pass the previous stage still as a reference and say "everything else identical". Regenerate the still, not the clip.
- **Video model adds text or subtitles:** add "no text, no captions, no letters" to the prompt and regenerate once.
- **Clip starts frozen:** add "motion starts on frame 1" and regenerate once.
- **Character lip-syncs:** add "the character does not talk; mouth closed".
- **Narration runs long:** cut words from the script; don't speed it up.
- **Balance too low for the quoted tier:** offer Lite, or fewer animated beats. Never start a run you can't finish.
