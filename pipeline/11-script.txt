Why This Castle Was Built Like a Maze
(F3, Himeji Castle; opening A, approved at Gate A on 18 Nov 2026; 277 words, ~79 s at 3.5 words/s)

If you build a castle on a hill, you see armies coming. But they see you too, and march straight up.

So you bend the path into a maze. It doubles back, so a 430-foot walk becomes over 1,000 feet, leading attackers away from the tower.

But they'll rush the gates. So no gate opens straight in. Each one forces a turn under your guns.

But they'll climb the walls. So the stone walls curve up like an open fan, until the top is almost straight up. And chutes drop stones on anyone who tries.

But enemy archers will pick off your soldiers. So you shoot back through holes shaped like circles, triangles and squares. These are loopholes.

But fire arrows can burn a wooden castle down. So you coat it all in white plaster that fire can't get through. That's why it's called the White Heron.

But what if they just wait for you to starve? So you dig 33 wells, and fill storehouses with rice and salt.

And the main tower hides a secret. Outside, it looks like five floors. Inside, there are seven, and two toilets in the basement for the siege. They were probably never used.

Because the siege never comes. Later, it's auctioned for 23 yen to a man who plans to tear it down. Too expensive. He leaves it.

In 1945, bombers burn most of the city around it. Workers hide the white tower under black nets. Locals say a firebomb hit the top floor and never went off.

This is Himeji Castle. Four hundred years later, it's still standing.

Bend the path, hide the floors, and let them get lost.
