Idea V1 (section 3), retitled at Gate A. Script approved at Gate A on 18 Nov 2026 (`12-script.txt`): 222 spoken words, est.
~63.4 s at 3.5 words/s, cold-open shape (the lid blasts at ~0.5 s; "It wasn't sewer gas" at ~3.7 s; the first "But" at ~10 s),
loop ending. Titles: YouTube "Why Some Manhole Covers Are Full of Holes"; TikTok "Why are some manhole covers full of holes? 💥"
+ #HowItWorks #Engineering #LearnOnTikTok #3DAnimation #Manhole #Winter. Comment question (caption or pinned comment, never

Est. seconds = words / 3.5. The real timings come from the narration (Step 6) and replace these; clip lengths are set from them.
Look (section 5): one sliced-open set seen by a travelling camera; every line is a clip, no planned holds; every still is an edit
of the wide (W0) or of a neighbouring position; captions ALL CAPS ~57 px at ~75% down; solid white arrows and thick rings; no labels.

## Set
- **Place:** a short stretch of a two-lane city street in winter, shown as a cutaway model: grey asphalt under a thin skin of
  grey-white slush, slush heaped along the curbs; a sidewalk with a granite curb on the left; plain brick and stone building
  fronts behind it with dark windows and no signs; one round iron manhole lid in the middle of the near lane, with one small pick
  hole and no lettering. Everything stands on a block of ground cut straight down like a cake toward the camera: its front face
  shows the road's layers (dark asphalt, grey concrete, brown packed earth) and, under the lid, the vault cut open so its inside
  shows. The lower ~40% of the wide frame is the plain cut face, for the captions.
- **The vault:** a rectangular concrete room about two metres deep under the lid, its front wall cut away: a short round concrete
  neck from the lid down into it; galvanised steel racks on the back and side walls holding thick black cables in heavy coats,
  looping round the room and into round duct openings in the side walls; a block of concrete ducts running off to the right under
  the street; a bare concrete floor. From S5 on, a small grey sensor box on the back wall, with a small light.
- **On the street:** an orange salt truck with no lettering in the far lane, its spinner throwing salt (W0, H0); two more round
  lids further along the street (seen from HIGH); one grey pigeon on our lid (the one small living thing).
- **Stage ladder:**
  - S0: the solid lid with slush on it and the pigeon standing on it; the vault clean and dim, the cables in thick black coats.
    **First frame (ST0).**
  - S1 (passing; B1-B2, B10): the blast: the lid flies off on a column of orange flame and grey smoke; then the open hole smokes.
  - S2: clear meltwater drips falling through the pick hole onto the cables (B5-B6).
  - S3: one cable's coat crusted white with salt along a crack (B7); the arc across the crack (B8, made in the clip).
  - S4: the vault full of grey smoke (B9).
  - S5: the vented lid: the same size, full of evenly spaced round holes; daylight through them from below; thin smoke streaming
    out (B12-B14); the sensor box on the wall (B15).
  - S6: small steel latches at four points round the rim; the lid lifts a finger's width with smoke puffing out (passing, B17)
    and settles back. **Last frame (ST9): S6 in frame 0's framing, the pigeon back on the lid.**
- **Grade:** bright, soft winter daylight from a low sun; the flame orange; the arc blue-white; the vault dimmer, lit from the
  cut face.
- **Water:** only grey-white slush and a few clear drips: no pools and no cyan water (the STYLE line without its water clause).
- **No people, no text, no logos:** the truck's cab windows are dark; no lettering on the lids (real ones carry the utility's
  name); no street signs, house numbers or steam stacks (New York's steam pipes are a different system).
