This frozen turkey just hit three-hundred-fifty-degree oil, and the pot erupted into a fireball. The oil didn't explode.
The ice inside the bird did.

As the ice melts, the water sinks, because it's heavier than oil. But at the hot bottom, it flashes to steam, and swells
about seventeen hundred times. It shoves the oil up and over the rim. This is a boil-over. And right under the pot is an
open flame. The oil hits it, and the whole thing goes up.

So you thaw the bird in the fridge, a day for every four or five pounds, and pat it dry. But even a thawed bird takes up
room. So you dunk it in water first, lift it out, and mark the line. That's all the oil you need. But a spill can still
reach the flame. So you shut off the gas, then lower the bird in slowly. But a deck is wood, and it's attached to your
house. So the fryer goes out on flat ground, far from both.

Here's the twist: Thanksgiving is the number one day of the year for cooking fires at home. That's why firefighters drop
frozen turkeys into fryers on the news every November. And your uncle? He swears this one's thawed. Thaw the bird, measure
the oil, and keep the fire in the yard.
