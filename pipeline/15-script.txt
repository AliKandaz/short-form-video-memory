Cut open this NFL field, and it's sitting on wheels. Five hundred and forty-six of them. And it's about to drive right out of
the stadium.

You're in the desert, so the roof closes against the heat. But grass under a roof can't get enough sun. So you grow the whole
field in one giant steel tray, and roll it outside. But the tray weighs nearly nineteen million pounds. So it rides on those
wheels, along thirteen steel rails. Only seventy-six have motors, one horsepower each: less power than most cars. So it crawls:
the trip outside takes over an hour. But a steel tray full of soil holds water like a bathtub. So the grass grows in sand, over
a drainage mat, with pipes that carry the water away. But a stadium can't sit empty all week while its grass is outside. So the
bare concrete floor hosts concerts, trade shows, even basketball's Final Four.

Here's the twist: this isn't an indoor field that goes out for sun. It lives outside most days, and moves in for games. That's
why, during the Final Four here, TV viewers could spot a football field parked outside. And this desert tortoise? It could
outrun the whole field. Roll it out, let it grow, and wheel it in for kickoff.
