### Facts checked (21 Nov 2026; web-search summaries, the pages can't be opened from a session)
- **Fifty more strings trip the breaker.** A 100-count incandescent mini string draws about 40 W (40–50 W); a 15-amp, 120-volt
  circuit carries 1,800 W, 1,440 W as a continuous load; 36 strings reach that and 51 draw about 2,040 W (Christmas Lights
  Etc, Mr. Electric, CoolToday, utility guides). The living room, dining room and front porch often share one 15-amp circuit
  (one guide), hence "the porch and the living room". The draft's "takes the oven with it" was wrong: an oven has its own
  circuit.
- **"About a tenth of the power."** 100-count LED mini strings draw 4–7 W against about 40 W (EnergySage, Govee, utility
  guides): a tenth to a sixth. The Department of Energy's own comparison, as widely quoted: about $0.27 to light a tree with
  LED C-9s for a season against about $10 with incandescent ones.
- **The outlet that shuts itself off.** A ground-fault interrupter trips when the current going out and coming back differ by
  4–6 mA, in a fraction of a second (the summaries give "within a tenth of a second"; the "1/40 of a second" often quoted
  wasn't found, so it isn't said). Outdoor lights belong on GFCI outlets, and rain in the connections is the usual cause of
  trips (Christmas Designers, Mr. Electric, The Christmas Light Emporium, forum reports). Sealed covers and drip loops are the
  usual fix.
- **Pixels.** A "smart pixel" is an addressable RGB light with its own chip (WS2811); a controller sends each one its colour
  and the show is sequenced to music in software such as xLights, 20–40 frames a second (instructables, Adafruit, hobby
  forums). The script says "in time with music" and gives no frame rate.
- **White turns pink.** When the voltage sags along a long run, full white looks pink at the far end (red needs the least);
  the fix is called power injection: feeding power into the middle or far end (Learn Christmas Lighting, the DIY Christmas
  wiki, ENTTEC's "pinking" note, forum reports). No "every N pixels" rule was found, so none is said.
- **The radio.** Shows send the music over a low-power FM transmitter and a yard sign gives the frequency (Angi; a Pullman,
  Washington show's sign says to tune to 95.5; others 93.5 and 95.3). The legal limit is a field strength of 250 microvolts a
  metre at 3 metres (47 CFR 15.239), about 200 feet of range (Angi, citing the FCC). The script gives no range.
- **The props.** Mega tree, arches and singing faces are the hobby's standard pieces and are sold as kits (Boscoyo Studio,
  HolidayCoro).
- **The bill and the nightly power:** `16-bill.md`.
