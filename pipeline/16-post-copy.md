Replies ready:
- "What's in the bill?" → One $3 string to start. Then 51 LED strings ($305), covers for the plug joins ($120), a pixel
  controller with ten strings of pixels ($715), power-injection parts ($52), an FM transmitter ($140), and the tree, the four
  arches and the snowman ($772). $2,107 in rough US prices, 2026. A bigger roof costs more.
- "Less than a dollar a night?" → About 19 to 96 cents for five hours, at the US average of about 18 cents a kWh. The same
  51 strings as old incandescent bulbs would be about $1.85 a night.
- "Why did the breaker trip?" → 51 old-style strings pull about 2,040 watts. A 15-amp circuit carries 1,800.
- "What's a ground-fault outlet?" → A GFCI. It compares the current going out with the current coming back, and trips when
  a few thousandths of an amp go missing, for example through rain in a plug.
- "Why did white turn pink?" → Voltage drop. Far down the wire the pixels get less voltage, blue and green fade first, and
  white goes pink. Feeding power into the middle of the run fixes it: that's power injection.
- "Is the radio legal?" → Low-power FM is allowed without a licence in the US under the FCC's Part 15 rule, at a strength
  that reaches about 200 feet: the cars out front, not the next street.
- "What station is 88.7?" → Made up for the video. A real show picks an empty spot on the dial in its own town.
- "What's the dog's name?" → That's up to you. Top comment wins.
- "What software runs the lights?" → Most home shows use xLights, which is free.
