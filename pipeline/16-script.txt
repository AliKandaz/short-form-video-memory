If you clip one string of lights to your gutter, you have a Christmas light show.

But you plug in fifty more strings, and the breaker trips: the porch and the living room go dark. So you swap every bulb for
an LED: the same glow, on about a tenth of the power. But the first rain gets into the plugs, and everything shuts off again.
The outlet felt power leaking and cut it before it could shock you. This is a ground-fault outlet. So every plug goes inside a
sealed box. But all your lights can do is blink. So every bulb gets its own tiny chip, and a controller sets each one's color
in time with music. These are pixels. But halfway along the roof, white turns pink: the power is fading down the wire. So you
feed fresh power into the middle of the line. This is power injection. But the music wakes the whole street. So you send it
out from a tiny radio transmitter, and every car out front tunes in. That's why light-show houses post a radio station on a
sign in the yard.

Add a tree made of lights, arches over the driveway, and a snowman that sings. That's your light show. The bill: about two
thousand dollars, and less than a dollar a night in power.
