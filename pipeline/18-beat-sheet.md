### Facts checked (25 Nov 2026, from web-search summaries; prices and their links are in `18-bill.md`)
- **Slipway:** a ramp on the shore on which ships are built, launched and hauled out (Wikipedia, "Slipway"). A track into
  the water with a wheeled cradle is a marine railway, or patent slip: cradle, track and hauling machinery (Wikipedia,
  "Patent slip" and "Ship cradle"; Pile Buck's guide to marine railways). Either name is right for what loop 1 builds.
- **Stern first:** in an end-on launch the hull goes down the ramp "usually stern first" (Wikipedia, "Ceremonial ship
  launching"; Britannica).
- **Launching triggers** hold the ship and, "when released at the moment of launching, permit the sliding ways to move over
  the standing ways" (Britannica, ship construction).
- **Drag chains:** "piles of chains are laid alongside the sides of the ship to act as drags" (Britannica); they slow a
  ship launched into a narrow waterway so that it does not strike the opposite bank (Wikipedia); "drag chains of
  predetermined weights are used to control the ship's entry into the water" (OSHA, shipbuilding). No source gave how much
  chain a hull of a given weight needs: the 2 tons in the bill are my sizing.
- **Ballast** is weight placed low in a ship to lower its centre of gravity; a light ship floats high and is easily pushed
  over; solid ballast has been shingle, sand, stone and iron bars (Wikipedia, "Ship ballast").
- **Boat hoist:** a travel lift is "also called a boat hoist, boat gantry crane, or boat crane"; it straddles the boat,
  passes slings under the hull and lifts (Wikipedia, "Travel lift"). Marine Travelift, Inc. holds the trademarks, so the
  video says "boat hoist". Hoists run out over the water on piers (Bellingham Marine: three fixed concrete piers, each
  carrying a travel lift; Shoreline Foundation: the piers that carry a 900-ton lift at Derecktor, Dania).
